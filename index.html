What is an overbounce?

An overbounce is a physics glitch in CoD4 where, instead of coming to a stop when you land on a slope, the game launches you into the air. Done right it can send you flying at speeds well beyond anything the movement system was designed to produce. This page explains why it happens, how to actually pull it off, and what we know for sure versus what's still a bit fuzzy.

The short version

Every time you're on the ground and moving against a slope, the game runs a little correction to smooth out your position, the same code that lets you walk up ramps without bouncing off them like a wall. That correction is supposed to slow you down gently. But the formula behind it was never clamped, so if the correction is big enough it can flip sign entirely. Instead of slowing you down it throws you upward (or sideways, if it's your horizontal speed that gets hit) at speeds close to what you were falling or running at.

It doesn't matter whether you're crouched or standing, the effect is identical either way. Going prone is a different story: the game re-checks your posture right before applying the bounce, and in practice that check almost always fails and cancels the whole thing out. That's why you'll basically never get an overbounce while prone, even though nothing in the code explicitly blocks it.

How to actually do one

You need a real slope, one you could normally walk up without sliding back down. A flat step or stair edge won't do it, the game would need you pushing into the vertical face of the next step up, which isn't really something you can set up on purpose.

The most reliable setup we've found is landing right on the edge of that slope, somewhere between about 0.125 and 5 units from the edge. Your feet clip the slope just long enough to trigger the correction, and then you immediately fall off the edge and keep falling. Here's what that looks like in an actual logged test: falling at roughly -2128 units/second vertically, the character's feet touch the edge for a single frame (the game registers you as grounded on something for that one frame, braking you down to -1461), and the very next frame it's airborne again with the vertical speed flipped from -1461 to +1610. (The full breakdown of why the flip took two frames in this case is on the technical page.)

Speed matters. Walking into a slope at normal speed does nothing, the correction is too small to flip sign either way. What your speed actually decides is how strong the launch is once the flip happens, not whether it happens at all. Whether you get an overbounce comes down to landing precisely on the edge; carrying more speed into that landing just makes the ones that do go off bigger. Usually that speed comes from a fall, though the same trick works with horizontal speed on flat ground right before an incline, in which case it's your sideways velocity that gets flipped instead of your vertical one.

Framerate is the other half of it. Dropping to a low FPS, 15 works well, makes the correction much stronger for the same speed. It doesn't have to be the exact instant you switch FPS, it just needs to already be low by the time you actually land on that edge.

FAQ

Does it work crouched? Yes, exactly the same as standing.

Is this the same as the classic Quake overbounce? No. The Quake version comes from a velocity rescale after a clip that CoD4 simply doesn't have in its equivalent code. Different bug, different engine path, they just look similar from the outside.

For the curious: how it actually works

It comes down to a step-correction formula in PM_StepSlideMove that's supposed to gently slow you down when the game repositions you onto a step or slope, but the scale factor it computes is never clamped. Push the correction far enough and that factor goes negative, flipping your velocity instead of shrinking it.
